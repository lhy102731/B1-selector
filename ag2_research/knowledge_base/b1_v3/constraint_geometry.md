# Constraint Geometry Report - What actually creates B1 V3 edge

This document summarises the closure of the question "where does B1 V3 alpha
come from?" using only evidence from Phase 12B, 12C, and 14B.

## TL;DR

B1 V3's edge is **not** produced by an entry generator. It is produced by the
intersection of about 9-13 filter constraints, with `C_wave_qualified`
operating as a candidate-volume generator and `t_plus_3_min_return` as the
sole independent exit alpha. The "alpha" lives in the *surface* of the joint
constraint polytope, not in any individual rule.

## 1. Why generator-only collapsed (Phase 12B)

Phase 12B disabled every entry-side filter except `require_wave_qualified=True`
and ran with default exits. Result:

| Window | baseline | alpha_only | Jaccard |
|---|---|---|---:|
| A_2023 | 94 / +12.9% / Sh 1.57 / PF 2.66 | 915 / +10.7% / Sh 0.68 / PF 1.16 | 0.051 |
| B_2024H1 | 36 / -1.9% / Sh -0.58 / PF 0.64 | 486 / -13.2% / Sh -3.17 / PF 0.65 | 0.034 |
| C_2024H2_latest | 144 / +7.1% / Sh 0.48 / PF 1.35 | 1802 / +14.2% / Sh 0.59 / PF 1.10 | 0.040 |

Take-aways:

- alpha-only is **not** baseline at a smaller scale - it is a different
  strategy. Trade-level Jaccard 3-5% means at least 95% of trades differ.
- Raw signal count inflated 30-40x; trade count inflated 10-20x; PF collapsed
  to 1.10-1.16; max drawdown roughly tripled.
- The wave generator alone permits ~8000 raw candidates in C; ranking and
  position limits do not save the system from trading noise.

Conclusion: a single "verified alpha component" produces noise, not edge.
The edge does not live inside the generator in isolation.

## 2. Why single ablation misled (Phase 12A vs 14B)

Phase 12A removed each constraint individually and ranked them by ΔRet.
Result: most constraints had |ΔRet| < 1 pp average and 6 had Jaccard 1.000 in
all three windows. Plausible-sounding conclusion: most filters are redundant.

Phase 14B revealed that conclusion was an artefact of one-at-a-time testing:

| Constraint | Phase 12A single ΔPF | Phase 14B remove-two int_PF |
|---|---:|---|
| B_KltD | 0.000 (always) | -0.94 paired with A_J_range |
| F_white_gt_yellow | -0.038 | -1.02 paired with A_J_range |
| A_J_range | +0.929 (anti-alpha alone) | dominant hub: -1.02, -0.94, -0.78 |
| F+A+B_KltD (triple) | n/a | int_PF -1.58 (stronger than any pair) |

Mechanism: with the full filter stack in place, downstream constraints
"shadow" upstream constraints. `B_KltD` has 0 effect alone because the J
range already excludes K>D cases. Once J range is removed, B_KltD becomes
binding and removing it then collapses PF by an additional ~1 unit.

This is why "redundant by single ablation" cannot be trusted: the apparent
redundancy was a side-effect of evaluating each filter under the *protection*
of all the others.

## 3. Why interaction dominates

The interaction matrix shows |interact| >> |single| for several constraint
pairs:

```
F_white_gt_yellow alone:                  ΔPF -0.04
A_J_range alone:                           ΔPF +0.93
F + A together  (expected additive 0.89):  observed ΔPF -0.13
=> interaction ΔPF -1.02

B_KltD alone:                              ΔPF  0.00
A_J_range alone:                           ΔPF +0.93
A + B_KltD together (expected +0.93):      observed ΔPF -0.02
=> interaction ΔPF -0.94
```

And the strongest three-way:

```
F + A + B_KltD removed together:           int_PF -1.58
(more negative than any pair)
```

This higher-order interaction means the constraint geometry has effective
dimension >= 3. The alpha is generated by a coordinate intersection, not by
a sum of independent factors.

## 4. Why trade identity cannot be factorized

Phase 12C greedily restored constraints to the alpha-only system in ΔJaccard
descending order:

| Step | Restored | A Jac | B Jac | C Jac |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | F | 0.087 | 0.078 | 0.068 |
| 2 | + A | 0.169 | 0.173 | 0.144 |
| 3 | + D | 0.297 | 0.378 | 0.256 |
| 4 | + F+A+D+E | 0.413 | 0.636 | 0.440 |
| 5 | + B_KltD | 0.413 | 0.636 | 0.440 |
| 6 | + G | 0.413 | 0.636 | 0.440 |
| 7 | + C (all 7 groups) | 0.419 | 0.636 | 0.476 |

Even with all 7 Phase 12C groups restored, Jaccard stalls at 0.42-0.64. PF
retention reaches 78-118% by step 4, but trade identity does not -
because the remaining 9 ungrouped filters (A2, B2 vol gates, B4, B5, B7,
B8, B9, C_no_wave_break) collectively control which trades survive even
after the top concentrators have re-narrowed the candidate pool.

The factoring question "which N constraints recover X% Jaccard" has the
answer:

| Target Jaccard | Required constraints |
|---|---|
| 50% | ~4 (F + A + D + E) |
| 70% | not reachable with 7 |
| > 80% | requires the full stack (~13 active filters) |

PF retention is approximately factorisable into the top 4-5 constraints.
**Trade identity is not.**

## 5. Why B1 V3 is a constraint geometry system

A "generator-driven" system has the property that:

- Removing the generator destroys the system.
- Keeping the generator and removing concentrators degrades performance
  proportionally to the concentrator weight.

A "constraint-geometry" system has these properties instead:

- Removing the generator does NOT alone produce a working subsystem.
- Single-filter removal under-states impact because downstream filters
  shadow upstream filters.
- Joint removals reveal interaction effects exceeding the sum of singles.
- Higher-order interactions (triples) can exceed pair interactions.
- Trade identity cannot be reproduced from a small subset of constraints.

All five properties match B1 V3 (Phase 12B + 12C + 14B). The system is
constraint-geometric.

## Hub constraints

A hub is a constraint whose removal triggers cascading changes across many
other constraints.

| Constraint | Negative pair count | Role |
|---|:-:|---|
| A_J_range | 3 (F+A, A+B_KltD, A+E) | HUB - appears in every top negative interaction |
| F_white_gt_yellow | 1 (F+A) | bridge to A |
| E_wave_healthy | 1 (A+E) | bridge to A |
| B_KltD | 1 (A+B_KltD) | leaf conditioned on A |
| D_white_slope | 0 | independent (substitutive only) |

A_J_range alone is "anti-alpha" by single-removal but is the structural
spine of the constraint geometry. This is the cleanest example of why
constraint-geometry systems cannot be reduced to single-component metrics.
